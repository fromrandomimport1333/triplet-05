# City Brain: detection module (report paragraph)

**What.** The detection module turns the validated stream of sensor readings and emergency calls into incidents (flood, fire, accident, traffic jam, power problem). It publishes a message only when an incident opens, changes or closes, never one per reading, so the LLM and the scoring model are called a few times per incident.

**How.** Each sensor keeps a running mean and variance (cumulative during a 20-reading warm-up, then an exponential moving average with weight 0.05). A reading is bad when it reaches the sensor's alert level from the city description, or lies more than three standard deviations away in the bad direction of its type. Three bad readings in a row make a sensor abnormal, three good ones make it normal again, and only quiet readings are learned, so a long flood or a slow ramp never becomes the new normal. Per zone and incident type, each source (one sensor, or all recent calls of one category in the zone) contributes p = min(cap, weight x strength x trust), and the confidence is the noisy-OR 1 - prod(1 - p). Strength is 1 at alert level, otherwise z/6, and for calls the number of calls over three. Guardian's verdict sets the trust: trusted 1.0 (or its score), suspicious 0.3, rejected 0, and 0.6 with `verified: false` when there is no verdict or Guardian is offline. Caps keep a single source low (0.6 for a reading, 0.55 for calls) unless it is a reading held at alert level (0.95). Support signals (a power spike for a fire, a neighbouring zone's water level) only count next to a primary one. An incident opens at confidence 0.5 on two checks in a row and closes below 0.25 on two checks or when its sensors fall silent. There is at most one open incident per zone and type, and updates are sent on new evidence or a severity change, at most every 5 s.

**Why.** The noisy-OR is easy to explain: independent signals that agree raise the confidence, and one weak signal cannot raise it alone. The caps are what make the attack scenarios fail. Ten fake fire calls count as one source and give at most one low-severity, unverified incident. A falsified water sensor that Guardian rejects counts for nothing, and the timeline says why. Rush hour slows every zone at once, so a slowdown seen in most zones is treated as a city pattern, not as a jam. A zone still counts as jammed if it is far slower than the city median. A sudden speed drop without any call stays below the open threshold, because it could be a jam as well as an accident. Every threshold is in `config/detection.yaml`.

**Limits.** The weights and thresholds are set by hand on the mock city, not fitted on data. Real sensors are noisier, and the warm-up, z-score and caps will need tuning at the joint run. Sources are assumed independent, which is not true for sensors that share a cause. During a city-wide slowdown, a jam is found only if it is much worse than the median. Event timestamps come from the Twin, so a clock skew of a few seconds shifts the durations. Detection keeps only a short memory (two minutes of calls, twelve readings per sensor) and restarts its baselines after a restart.
